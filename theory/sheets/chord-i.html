WHY MOST MUSIC STARTS ON CHORD I: 'Home Base'

Chord I (the tonic) is the home chord of the key. Starting on it instantly tells the listener where 'home' is, makes the key feel stable, and gives every later chord meaning as tension or movement away from that home base.

Chord I Is the Tonic (The 'Home' Chord)

In tonal music, the tonic is the centre of gravity. It's where melodies feel settled, and where cadences like to land.

  • I = stability
  • Other chords = movement away
  • Returning to I = resolution

Reason #1: It Establishes the Key Immediately

Starting on I is like saying the title of the story on page one. The listener's brain locks onto the key centre fast.

  • You instantly hear: "we're in C major" (or whatever)
  • Later chords sound meaningful because home is clear

Reason #2: It Feels Conclusive and Safe

I contains the most important notes of the scale (1–3–5). Those notes feel like 'resting points'.

  • Melodies often start on chord tones
  • Bass on the tonic feels grounded
  • It avoids sounding 'unfinished'

Reason #3: It Makes Tension & Release Work

Tension only means something if we know what 'release' is. Starting on I sets the reference.

  • IV feels like moving out
  • V feels like pulling back
  • I feels like arrival

Reason #4: It's Practical for Singers and Bands

In many styles, starting on I helps everyone enter confidently.

  • Clear pitch reference for the singer
  • Clear root for the bass
  • Clear groove for the rhythm section

But Not ALL Music Starts on I

Composers sometimes start elsewhere for drama, surprise, or ambiguity.

  • Starting on V = instant tension
  • Starting on vi = 'sad' colour before the key is confirmed
  • Starting on ii/IV = floating, prelude-like feel

Mini Experiment (60 Seconds)

Try this on piano in C major:

  • Start: C → F → G → C (feels stable)
  • Now start on: G → C (tension then release)
  • Now start on: Am → F → G → C (sad colour then home)

Why Those Exceptions Still Work

Even if the first chord isn't I, the music usually confirms I soon—so the listener still gets a home base.

  • Early cadence to I
  • Melody emphasises tonic notes
  • Bass motion points to tonic

CHEAT SHEET: Why Start on I

  • I = tonic = home base.
  • It establishes the key instantly.
  • It feels stable and complete.
  • It makes later chords feel like 'movement'.
  • Non-I openings usually confirm I soon.

Common Mistakes

  • Thinking 'starting on I' is a rule (it's a convention)
  • Ignoring how quickly music confirms the tonic
  • Assuming non-I openings mean 'different key' (often they don't)
