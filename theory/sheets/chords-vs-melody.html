PIANO PRACTICE DAILY

CHORDS vs MELODY: Background Colour + Foreground Story

Most music can be understood as two layers working together: chords and melody. Chords create the colour, mood, and harmonic 'landscape'—like the lighting and scenery in a film—while the melody is the narrative line your ear follows, like the main character speaking.

What a Melody Is (The Narrative)

A melody is a single line of notes that moves through time. It's the part you hum, sing, or remember.

  • It has direction (up/down)
  • It has rhythm (how long notes last)
  • It creates phrases like musical sentences

What Chords Are (The Landscape)

Chords are groups of notes played together (or implied) that support the melody. They set the emotional colour underneath.

  • Major vs minor changes mood instantly
  • 7ths, sus chords, extensions add flavour
  • Chord changes create the sense of motion

Why Chords Feel Like 'Background'

Even when you don't notice them, chords shape how the melody feels.

  • Same melody + different chords = different emotion
  • Chords create tension and release
  • They tell your ear where 'home' is

Why Melody Feels Like 'Foreground'

Our brains naturally latch onto one voice as the 'speaker'.

  • Highest line often sounds like the melody
  • Clear rhythm = easy to follow
  • Repetition makes it memorable

A Film Analogy (Easy to Remember)

If music were a movie:

  • Chords = lighting, scenery, atmosphere
  • Bass line = the ground the scene stands on
  • Melody = the main character / dialogue
  • Rhythm = the pacing and editing

How They Work Together

Great music is the relationship between the two layers.

  • Melody lands on chord tones (stable moments)
  • Melody uses non-chord tones (tension)
  • Then resolves back into the chord (release)

Mini Practice (90 Seconds)

1) Pick a simple melody (Twinkle Twinkle). 2) Play it alone. 3) Add basic chords underneath (I–IV–V–I). 4) Now change ONE chord to minor and listen. You'll hear: same story, different scenery.

Piano Tip: Make the Melody Sing

On piano, you often play both layers at once—so balance matters.

  • Play melody louder, chords softer
  • Use legato for melody, lighter touch for accompaniment
  • Think: 'story in the right hand, scenery in the left'

CHEAT SHEET: Story vs Scenery

  • Melody = the line you remember (narrative).
  • Chords = mood, colour, and harmonic landscape.
  • Same melody + new chords = new emotion.
  • Good music = tension (non-chord tones) → release (chord tones).
  • On piano: melody louder, chords softer.

Common Mistakes

  • Playing chords too loud (melody disappears)
  • Treating chords as 'just filler' (they shape everything)
  • Ignoring non-chord tones (that's where expression lives)
