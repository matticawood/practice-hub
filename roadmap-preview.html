The model

Each skill has a master progression (an ordered list). A level's baseline covers a prefix; its Extras are simply "the next items in the list." So a First Steps keen bean climbs D, F, A, B♭, E♭… forever; an advanced Intermediate reaches toward Confident targets; a less-advanced member always sees one small next step. Prescriptive (named scales, not "all of them") and bridging (every Extra steps toward the next level and past it).

Depth scales with the hours-gap to the next stage (that's the time to fill):

Higher levels can't lean on scale coverage (it runs out), so they lean on rising score targets, repertoire volume and repertoire difficulty to stay bottomless.

Master progressions (correct the ORDER, it's your call)

  • Major scales (HT, 2 oct): C · G · D · F · A · B♭ · E♭ · E · A♭ · B · D♭ · G♭
  • Minor scales (nat + harm + mel): A · E · D · B · G · C · F♯ · C♯ · F · B♭ · G♯ · E♭
  • Arpeggios: follow the major-scale order, then minors in the same order.
  • Note Recognition target ladder: 10 · 14 · 18 · 22 · 26 · 30 · 34 · 38 · 40 · 44 · 48; difficulty climbs: in a key → across the keys → mixed clef → with accidentals.
  • Chord Recognition: triads in a key → triads free → + inversions → sevenths → sevenths + inversions; scores rise 8–12 within each.
  • Ear Training: maj vs min → triads → sevenths → progressions; scores rise 10–16.
  • Theory (levels 1–5): baseline completes N; Extra = begin N+1, then complete N+1.
  • Sight-reading (minutes): Extras raise the difficulty band (below standard → at standard) and add minute milestones.
  • Improv (hours): triads + melody → add 7ths → over ii–V–I → in a chosen key → trading 4s over a backing.
  • Repertoire (tiers 0–5: Absolute Beginner…Expert): add count at the tier, then reach up a tier.

Per-level breakdown

Each line: baseline → tier 1 → tier 2 → …

1 · First Steps (0–50 h · before Grade 1)

  • Scales: C, G → D → F → A → B♭ → E♭
  • Note Recognition: 10 (C, G) → 12 → 14 → 16 → 18 (adds D, F)
  • Repertoire: 1 Absolute Beginner piece → 2nd → 3rd → a first Beginner-tier piece
  • Theory: begin Level 1 → complete Level 1
  • Sight-reading: 500 min → 750 → 1,000 min

2 · Beginner (50–150 h · around Grade 1–2)

  • Scales: C, G, D, F → A → B♭ → E♭ → E → A♭
  • Arpeggios: C, G, D, F → A → B♭ → E♭
  • Note Recognition: 18 (CGDF) → 22 → 26 → 30 → 34 (mixed clef)
  • Chord Recognition: 10 (CGDF triads in key) → 12 → triads free, 10
  • Ear Training: 12 (maj/min, CGDF) → 14 → 16 → triads, 12
  • Improv: 3 h → +2 h with 7ths → +2 h over ii–V–I
  • Theory: complete Level 1 → begin Level 2
  • Repertoire: 4 Beginner pieces → 5th → 6th → a Lower Intermediate stretch
  • Sight-reading: 1,000 min → 1,500 → 2,000 min

3 · Foundations (150–350 h · around Grade 3–4)

  • Scales: A, B♭, E♭ → E → A♭ → B → D♭ → G♭ (all 12 major)
  • Arpeggios: A, B♭, E♭ → E → A♭ → B → D♭ → G♭
  • Minor scales: A, E, D → B → G → C
  • Note Recognition: 18 (A, B♭, E♭) → 24 → 30 → 34 (mixed clef + accidentals)
  • Chord Recognition: 10 (triads free) → + inversions, 9 → 12
  • Ear Training: 16 (triads) → sevenths, 10 → 12
  • Theory: complete Level 2 → begin Level 3
  • Repertoire: 1–2 Lower Intermediate → 3rd → 4th → an Upper Intermediate stretch
  • Sight-reading: 2,000 min → 3,000 → 3,500 min (at standard)

4 · Intermediate (350–700 h · around Grade 5)

Recommendation: make the baseline prescriptive here. "All major scales" → the 12 named (all covered by Foundations Extras), and use Extras to push speed / thirds & sixths, not just coverage.
  • Scales: all 12 major, HT 2 oct → at ♩=88 even quavers → in sixteenths → a 3rd / a 6th apart → contrary motion from C
  • Arpeggios: all major → all minor (same key order) → dominant 7ths, common keys
  • Minor scales: B, G, C → F♯ → C♯ → F → B♭ → G♯ → E♭ (all 12 minor)
  • Note Recognition: 34 → 36 → 38 → 40
  • Chord Recognition: 9 (triads + inversions) → sevenths, 7 → 10
  • Ear Training: 12 (sevenths) → 14 → progressions, 10
  • Improv: +5 h → +3 h in a minor key → +3 h trading 4s over a backing
  • Theory: complete Level 3 → begin Level 4
  • Repertoire: 5 intermediate pieces → 6th → 7th → Upper Intermediate stretch → Advanced stretch
  • Sight-reading: 3,500 min → 5,000 → 6,000 min

5 · Confident (700–1,200 h · around Grade 6–7)

  • Scales: all maj+min, every key, 16ths @ 88 → @ 100 → @ 112 → in thirds → in sixths → chromatic + whole-tone
  • Arpeggios: all maj+min → dominant 7ths, all keys → diminished 7ths, all keys
  • Note Recognition: 40 → 44 → 48 → sight-read speed drill
  • Chord Recognition: 7 (sevenths free) → 10 → sevenths + inversions, 8
  • Ear Training: 14 (sevenths) → 16 → progressions, 10 → 12
  • Theory: complete Level 4 → begin Level 5
  • Repertoire: 6 Upper Intermediate → 7th → 8th → Advanced stretch → a 2nd Advanced piece
  • Sight-reading: 5,000 min → 6,500 → 8,000 min

6 · Advanced (1,200–2,000 h · around Grade 8)

Baseline is thin here; the 800 h gap is carried by repertoire volume & difficulty.
  • Scales: all fluent, every key → at concert tempo → double thirds (easy keys) → octaves HT → in tenths
  • Arpeggios: all fluent → dom + dim 7ths at tempo → a tenth apart
  • Musicianship: complete Theory Level 5 → analyse a piece you're learning (harmonic outline)
  • Repertoire: 6 Advanced → 7th → 8th → a first Expert stretch → a 2nd Expert piece → an Advanced work from memory
  • Sight-reading: 8,000 min → 10,000 → 12,000 → 15,000 min (accompaniment)
  • Performance: improvise a cadenza-style passage in a piece's style → perform one Advanced piece to standard

7 · Performer (2,000–3,500 h · diploma standard)

  • Repertoire: Advanced programme + 5 adv-to-expert → +1 Expert → +2 → +3 → a full contrasting recital set
  • Performance: programme from memory → perform to an audience / record it → a themed programme (one composer or era)
  • Sight-reading: 15,000 min → accompany another player/singer → read an orchestral reduction
  • Musicianship: transpose a piece at sight → harmonise a given melody → improvise in a named style

8 · Artist (3,500 h+ · top stage, open-ended)

  • Repertoire: full Expert programme + an Expert work of your own → a 2nd Expert work → a large-scale work (sonata/suite) → commission or arrange a piece
  • Performance: give a full recital → a themed recital → teach/coach the piece to someone else
  • Musicianship: improvise a complete piece → compose a short original → arrange for another instrument
