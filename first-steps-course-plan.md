# First Steps: the Absolute Beginners short course (design draft, v2)

Status: draft for review, revised to align with the Theory course, the roadmap and your ethos. Nothing built or notated yet.

House rules followed throughout: "stave" not "staff", "practice" always with a c, no "met/meet" for encountering a concept, no em dashes, no emojis, skills-not-grades framing.

## What this is and why it exists

An absolute beginner (never touched a piano) currently has nowhere to start inside the app. The library has only three pieces at difficulty 0, and even those assume you can already read. The genuine first-rung material (hand shape, first notes, first rhythms) lives inside copyrighted method books, so we cannot curate it for free. So we build it.

Its job is narrow and clear: **get a complete beginner to the point where they can play the curated beginner pieces** (Mikrokosmos Vol 1, Bartok For Children, Kabalevsky Op. 39, Bach's Little Preludes, Burgmuller Op. 100). Nothing more. It is deliberately short and finishable, ending on "Congratulations, you're ready for beginner pieces."

It is **not** an ear or improvisation course. Those get their own dedicated courses (Ear Training Levels 1 to 5, Improvisation Levels 1 to 5) alongside the Theory course, so First Steps stays focused on getting hands to the keys and reading enough to play.

## How it sits with the Theory course

First Steps is the **"learn to play"** partner to the Theory course's **"learn to read"**. Theory Level 1 already teaches reading thoroughly and hands-on (middle C as the anchor, the G-clef and F-clef landmark notes, the mnemonics, note and rest values, simple time), and the First Steps roadmap stage literally lists "Begin Theory Level 1" as an objective. So a beginner does both together: Theory Level 1 for reading and understanding, First Steps for the physical act of playing.

That means First Steps does **not** re-teach reading as a parallel (and possibly conflicting) system. It uses Level 1's exact reading approach and applies it at the keyboard. Its own backbone is the physical, coordination ladder that Theory does not systematically build: posture and a relaxed wrist, a steady pulse, one hand then the other, hands together, hand independence, and first real pieces.

## Two things taught up front (before Piece 1)

**Reading is not sight-reading.** Reading is working a piece out and learning to play it well: you go slowly, stop, and repeat as much as you like. Sight-reading is playing something new in a single pass without stopping. This course is entirely about the first one. Sight-reading is a separate skill with its own place in practice later.

**How to practice (the process, then reinforced in every piece):**
- Go slower than feels necessary.
- If something trips you, stop, find the exact note or beat that went wrong, and repeat just that small part slowly before running the whole thing again. Playing straight through is not practice.
- Count out loud to keep the pulse steady.
- If you cannot yet recognise a note on sight, say its name out loud as you play it, using the landmarks and the mnemonics from Theory Level 1, or read by shape: one step up, one step down.
- You do not have to do both at once. **Pick the one you are struggling with most.** If the timing is the problem, prioritise counting out loud. If the notes are the problem, prioritise saying the note names. Add the other back once the first is comfortable.

## The teaching approach

Designed backwards from what the curated pieces above actually demand, so the course installs exactly those prerequisites and nothing extra: reading anchored on middle C and the clef landmarks (in step with Level 1), a steady pulse through the note values Level 1 introduces, one hand then hands together building to real independence, and a first taste of shaping a line with touch and dynamics.

Design rule held throughout: **one genuinely new thing per piece**, everything else is consolidation. A beginner who meets a new rhythm, a new note range and hands-together in one piece drowns. Depth over breadth, one variable at a time.

Reinforced in **every** piece, because these are your First Steps priorities (habit, comfort, steady pulse, stop-and-fix):
- **A relaxed hand and a steady pulse** as the physical baseline.
- **The practice process above** (slow, isolate, count out loud or say the note names, whichever you are struggling with).
- **A daily-habit nudge** ("finish wanting to come back tomorrow"), since consistency is the real First Steps goal.

## The 12-piece ladder

Each row introduces one new **playing** skill and consolidates everything before it. Reading stays in step with Theory Level 1 (same landmarks, same mnemonics). Working titles.

| # | Working title | Key / position | Rhythm | Hands | The one new thing (playing) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Finding Your Way | White and black keys (no notation yet) | Steady beat | Either hand | Posture, relaxed wrist, finger numbers, finding notes, keeping a pulse |
| 2 | Middle C | Middle C up, RH | Quarter + half | RH alone | Playing single notes with the right hand; reading from the middle C landmark |
| 3 | Left Hand Joins | Middle C down, LH | Whole note added | LH alone | The left hand; reading down from middle C into the bass |
| 4 | Mirror Image | Both thumbs on middle C | Same as before | Hands together (contrary) | Hands together, gentlest form: symmetric mirror motion |
| 5 | Steps and Skips | C-E-G, RH then both | Consolidate | Hands together | Playing and reading a skip (a 3rd) as well as a step |
| 6 | A Little Waltz | C position | 3/4, dotted half | RH melody + one LH note per bar | Playing in 3/4, counting in three |
| 7 | Smooth and Singing | C position | Consolidate | Hands together | Legato touch, a two-note slur, louder and softer (shaping a line) |
| 8 | Quicker Steps | C position | Eighth notes | RH alone | Playing eighth notes evenly, counting the subdivision |
| 9 | Hands Apart | C, RH melody over held C-G | Consolidate | Independent | Hand independence: the two hands doing different jobs at once |
| 10 | Beyond the Position | C major, small shift | Consolidate | Hands together | Moving out of one fixed hand position |
| 11 | Rests and the Upbeat | C major | Rest, tie, anacrusis | Hands together | Playing silence, ties, and starting off the beat |
| 12 | Your First Piece (graduation) | C major | Combines all | Independent | Integration + your first C major scale. Hands off to the curated beginner set. |

Notes on a few choices:

- **Piece 1 has no notation.** It is orientation: how to sit, a relaxed hand and loose wrist, finger numbers, finding notes by the black-key groups, and keeping a steady pulse. It gets them comfortable at the keys before a note is read.
- **Piece 4 uses contrary motion from middle C** (both thumbs on C, both hands moving outward with the same fingers). Gentlest possible hands-together, no position change, and it reinforces reading both staves at once.
- **Piece 9 is the pivot.** Melody in the right hand over a held C and G in the left is the first time the hands disagree, and it is what the curated pieces most depend on. It gets its own step and a "left hand until automatic, then add the right" practice tip.
- **Piece 12 introduces the C major scale.** It plants the scale and sets the real goal, which lives on the roadmap: over the First Steps stage, build **C and then G major, hands together, two octaves**. No arpeggios at this stage (those begin at Beginner), no five-finger-pattern framing.

Deliberately **excluded** to keep it one thing at a time: chords and harmony theory, arpeggios, five-finger technical drills, keys beyond C major, sight-reading pressure, and ear training and improvisation (both have their own dedicated courses coming alongside Theory).

## The explainer for each piece

A consistent small frame per page:

1. **The one new thing** (the playing skill, one line).
2. **How to do it** physically (hands, fingers, relaxed wrist, touch).
3. **The reading or rhythm point** in this piece, tied to what Level 1 teaches.
4. **Keep the pulse** (count out loud).
5. **How to practice this** (slow, isolate the trouble spot, and count *or* name the notes depending on what you are struggling with).
6. **What it sets up** next.

### Sample explainer, Piece 2 "Middle C"

> **New:** your first note to play, and your anchor. Middle C is the note you always find your bearings from, on the keyboard and on the page.
>
> **How:** right-hand thumb on middle C, hand lightly curved, wrist loose. Let the weight of the arm play the note rather than pressing with the finger.
>
> **Reading:** every note here is middle C or a step above it. Read by shape, one step up at a time, so your fingers climb with the notes (thumb, 2, 3). If you cannot place a note yet, say its name out loud, using the landmarks and mnemonics from Theory Level 1.
>
> **Keep the pulse:** count out loud, a steady "1, 2, 3, 4", so the beat stays even.
>
> **How to practice:** play it slowly, find the one spot your hand hesitates, and play just that spot a few times before you run the whole thing again. That is practice. Playing it top to bottom over and over is not. Struggling with the timing? Prioritise counting out loud. Struggling with the notes? Prioritise saying their names.
>
> **Next:** the left hand does the same thing, one octave lower.

### Sample explainer, Piece 9 "Hands Apart" (the hard one)

> **New:** your two hands do different jobs at the same time. This is the skill every real piece is built on.
>
> **How:** the left hand holds one shape, C and G together, and stays put. The right hand plays the tune on top. The left hand is your anchor, the right hand is the voice.
>
> **Reading:** nothing new to read. Everything here you learned in earlier pieces, kept easy on purpose so the only new thing is the coordination.
>
> **How to practice:** left hand alone until you can hold it without looking. Only then add the right hand, slower than feels necessary. If it falls apart, your hands are ahead of you: halve the speed and count out loud.
>
> **Next:** now that the hands are independent, we can leave the safety of one hand position.

## Where it lives and how it behaves

### Home: Courses

The course lives in **Courses**, as the sibling to the Theory course. Theory teaches reading the page, First Steps teaches playing. Anyone can open and take it from Courses regardless of level, which covers the "available to anyone who wants it" case. Access is open; only the *dashboard suggestion* is gated.

### Delivery (reusing existing machinery)

- Each of the 12 pieces is **one lesson** in the First Steps course, reusing the Theory course's lesson and progress machinery (already member-facing, already tracks per-item completion, already has a player). Member access and completion tracking come for free.
- **Notation is a LilyPond-engraved image** embedded per lesson, produced by your sight-reading generator (correct grand stave, voices, fingering, dynamics). The course engine never lays out a stave, so its grand-stave rendering problems never enter the picture. Image blocks are already a supported lesson content type.
- **Audio** is included from the start (it already renders in the Theory course). Quality matters more here than anywhere, because a beginner imitates the reference, so the plan is: build with MIDI from the LilyPond source as a placeholder, then **replace each with Matthew's own recording** for release. One clean recording per piece at a steady learning tempo, human touch and phrasing, so it truly represents the piece.
- **Completion** = each lesson marked done; course complete when all 12 are done. That flag flips the dashboard.

### Dashboard states (the single Matt's Picks entry)

Matt's Picks is kept. For a flagged absolute beginner it holds **one** entry:

- **Not started:** "First Steps: start here" (the single pick). No repertoire picks, no method-book shelf to go buy. One door.
- **In progress:** "Continue First Steps, piece N of 12." **Sticky**: once started it does not disappear on its own.
- **Completed:** the entry flips and the beginner area **populates with the curated beginner pieces**. They graduate straight into beginner and never see the old three repertoire picks as a novice.

### Roadmap objectives

The course is also a **First Steps stage objective**, sitting alongside the existing "Begin Theory Level 1" and "Learn your first Absolute Beginner piece" goals. So the stage goal is, in effect, complete the First Steps course plus Theory Level 1. This is how the reading partner is surfaced: both are real objectives, not just a card pointer.

Crucially, **dismissing the course via the escape hatch ticks the objective off**, exactly as completing it does. A capable player who skips it is beyond it, so it should not sit on their roadmap as an unfinished goal. Completing it ticks it the normal way.

### The beginner gate (who sees the course as their default)

Show First Steps as the sole pick only when the member looks like a beginner on **every** signal. Concrete thresholds, anchored to the roadmap's own numbers (Beginner stage starts at 50 hours; Note Recognition target is 10 at First Steps and 18 at Beginner; Chord/Ear/Interval targets are 10 to 12 at Beginner):

- **Total practice under 5 hours** (logged minutes + prior-hours baseline minutes < 300), the "barely begun" floor, **and**
- **roadmap stage = First Steps** (level 0), **and**
- **no meaningful game score:** best Note Recognition under 8 in every key, and no Chord Recognition, Ear Training or Interval Training score at or above 4.

The game scores are the disambiguator: a decent Note Recognition score means they can read, any real Chord Recognition score means they definitely can, and Ear Training progress means they are hearing harmony. Anyone who has touched the games is excluded automatically. The total-practice floor excludes anyone who reported real experience in onboarding or has put in genuine time. This also keeps **existing members safe**: they already have logged practice, game scores or a higher roadmap level, so they are never dropped into the course. Thresholds are deliberately conservative and easy to tune once we see real numbers.

### The escape hatch (never trap anyone)

- Whenever the course is shown, it carries a visible secondary line: "Already play a bit? Jump to beginner pieces." One tap populates their card with the curated set, **ticks off the "Complete the First Steps course" objective** (they are beyond it), and can nudge them to set prior hours so their roadmap level corrects too.
- Background signals only choose the **first** suggestion. Once a member has started the course, progress is theirs and nothing removes it automatically. A strong game score two pieces in does not yank the course away; it only surfaces the escape hatch more plainly, so the member decides. The gate suggests, the member decides.

### The current absolute-beginner pieces

The existing difficulty 0 and 1 pieces stay fully browsable in the Library for anyone who wants to consolidate. They are simply no longer surfaced as dashboard picks for a true novice.

## Decisions settled

- **Name: First Steps.** Nice synergy with the First Steps stage, since it is the first absolute-beginner thing you do on the app.
- **First scale: C major**, introduced in Piece 12; the stage goal (built over First Steps) is C then G, hands together, two octaves. Exact fingering to be matched to Theory Level 1 and the roadmap scale objectives when Piece 12 is drafted.
- **Reading vs sight-reading distinction** taught up front, per your articles.
- **Ear and improv excluded**, covered by their own upcoming courses.
- **Gate thresholds set** (see the beginner gate section): under 5 hours total practice, roadmap stage First Steps, no meaningful game score. Conservative and tunable.
- **Audio from the start**, built on MIDI as a placeholder then replaced with Matthew's own recordings for release.
- **Length: 12 as the working number**, flexed only if drafting reveals a genuine prerequisite gap.
- **Objectives:** the course becomes a First Steps stage objective alongside "Begin Theory Level 1" and "Learn your first Absolute Beginner piece". Both completing AND dismissing it tick the objective off. (This resolves the companion question: reading is surfaced as its own objective, not just a card line.)
