## The six pillars (ranked by weight in the corpus)

### 1. Practising is not playing — and most people never actually practise
The single most-repeated theme. Runs through MMT 3, 24, 41, 54, 57, 72, 77, 82, 93, 103, 105, 107, 118.
- "Practicing is separate from playing and practicing is much more effective for improvement." (MMT 103)
- Run-throughs are a waste: "each mistake is played 28 times in that session without any targeted fixing." (MMT 103) · "playing through the piece of music - this is a complete waste of time." (MMT 24)
- Plan before you touch a key: "plan what you are going to practice before you actually play any key on the piano." (MMT 3)
- Fail-and-isolate: "isolate the mistake and only play up until one note beyond that mistake." (MMT 54)
- Repetition only counts if correct: "Every repetition strengthens that brain pathway, whether it is correct or incorrect." (MMT 118)
- "Practice is there to create change. Repetition is there to consolidate that change." (MMT 118)
- "We're practicing on behalf of all future pieces of music that we learn." (MMT 118)

### 2. It's music, not notes — musicality first, technique second
MMT 4, 36, 37, 53, 58, 59, 85, 94, 106, 111, 114, 115, 116.
- "Learning the notes makes up 40%-50% of music, but the remainder is made up of HOW you play those notes." (MMT 59)
- "learning to play the piano, rather than learning to play music through the piano." (MMT 36)
- "solve the musicality issue first... and then let the technique be guided, informed and layered on top." (MMT 37)
- The middle step: "1. What are the notes 2. What do those notes mean? 3. Play the notes in a way that reflects step 2." (MMT 94)
- "bash through the notes... in the hopes that they will go back and make those notes sound nice afterwards - this never actually happens." (MMT 72)
- "dynamics are best thought of as 'sentiments' rather than 'volume'." (MMT 106)

### 3. Consistency beats intensity — and the habit is the real beginner skill
MMT 1, 8, 41, 52, 69, 81, 92, 93, 100, 113.
- "Practicing for 10 minutes per day is much better than practicing for 4 hours once per week and eventually quitting." (MMT 52)
- Undercommit at first: "if you can practice 5 days a week, do 4... if you can do 40 minutes, do 30." (MMT 1)
- "do the absolute minimum to keep the dream alive." (MMT 41) · "depositing $1 into the bank." (MMT 41)
- Identity-based: "If you tell yourself that you are the type of person who practises the piano daily, then it becomes non-negotiable." (MMT 92)
- "make your main goal be persistence itself." (MMT 69)
- End on a win: "Leave something unfinished on purpose... keen to get back the next day." (MMT 93) · a good session is "one where you felt happy or even excited to practise again tomorrow." (MMT 93)

### 4. Theory isn't optional homework — it IS the music
MMT 18, 30, 57, 66, 74, 96.
- "Music theory is a description of music, not a prescription... Learning music theory is simply understanding what you are actually doing." (MMT 74)
- "Everything that you learn to do in music is music theory." (MMT 57)
- Feeling trapped = "not knowing enough music theory to understand that everything is an option!" (MMT 74)
- "understanding how music is constructed allows you more freedom with your instrument." (MMT 18)

### 5. Depth over breadth — master a few things deeply
MMT 60, 65, 70, 79, 102, 108.
- "learning just a handful of scales very well will be much more efficient than learning 20 scales on a surface level." (MMT 60)
- "You pick one pattern and you use it for absolutely everything until you don't have to think about it." (MMT 60)
- Right difficulty (n+1 / Goldilocks): "aiming for being able to play 80%-95% correctly." (MMT 108)
- Understanding ≠ playing: knowing what a triad is vs "being able to play all triads without hesitation is another." (MMT 8)

### 6. Progress isn't linear — judge it over months, welcome the mistakes
MMT 8, 18, 49, 52, 98, 100, 101, 113.
- "compare your playing to where it was a year ago, rather than where it was a day ago." (MMT 18)
- "'off days' are not a loss of skill... simply days where it is more difficult to access what you already know." (MMT 113)
- Negativity bias: "As you get better you aren't making more mistakes... you are comparing yourself to a new standard." (MMT 100)
- A note isn't a mistake until you react: "Notes only become mistakes if you react to them by losing control." (MMT 49)
- "Getting something wrong is an incredible opportunity because it shows you exactly what to practice." (MMT 54)

## Self-taught-specific positions (for THIS video's audience)
MMT 6, 20, 57, 108.
- Piano is not one skill: "learning the piano to be one skill, when in reality it's a set of skills." (MMT 6) — the five: sight reading, improvisation, theory, scales/technique, ear training (MMT 108).
- "a teacher CAN drastically increase your rate of progression, but you don't NEED a teacher." (MMT 20)
- No talent myth: "there is no such thing as talent or prodigies on an instrument is a hill that I am firmly willing to die on." (MMT 57)
- The self-taught risk: "there is no clear feedback loop to help course-correct in the way that a teacher would." (MMT 108) — so record yourself and interrogate mistakes.

## Supporting positions (strong, secondary)
- **Sight-reading:** reading ≠ sight-reading (MMT 6/24); read it once only, it's a saturation game, 70-90% accuracy (MMT 14/29/54); pattern recognition + landmark notes, not mnemonics (MMT 14/17/60).
- **Technique = relaxation, not strength:** "you relax your fingers to be curved, not curve them to be relaxed." (MMT 104) · weak little finger is anatomy — support it with wrist/arm, don't strengthen it (MMT 97/104).
- **Hands together, not separate:** "music doesn't exist as two separate hands... it exists as a series of events." (MMT 117) · hands-separate is only a repair tool (MMT 50/117).
- **Slow down:** "Speed is a byproduct of being incredibly comfortable... not something that should be a goal in itself." (MMT 99) · slow practice learns it faster long-term (MMT 86).
- **Ear/improv (opt-in, per Matt's own correction — not a universal beginner objective):** playing by ear = theory + pattern recognition, find the scale first (MMT 5/87); improv = applied theory, recombining drilled patterns, limit to 1-3 ideas (MMT 22/40/70).

## Selected points for the video "X Pieces of Invaluable Piano Advice for Self-Taught Beginners"

2. Getting the notes right is only half the job — Pillar 2

4. Don't run it through — isolate the mistake and fix that — Pillar 1
5. Theory isn't boring homework, it's the music itself — Pillar 4
6. Master a few things deeply instead of collecting many — Pillar 5
