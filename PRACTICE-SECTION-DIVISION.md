# The Practice section — division of labour

Drafted against the **live** site, not dev, so the inventory is what members
are using today.

---

## 1 · What is actually on each tab now

**Dashboard** — Your Path (level, ladder, hours). Progress by area (four bars).
Today / Your day at the piano (5 task cards). This level (16 objectives, 4
shown). Matt's picks. Community (activity feed + community posts). Your
leaderboard positions (5 cells). Your progress / At a glance (streak,
fortnight, this week, all time). Goals (weekly practice). Learn (2 courses +
5 practice tools). From Matt (live streams, next event, Matt's content).
Go deeper (article of the day, term of the day, weekly practice focus).

**Stats** — All-time practice. Pieces. Articles read. Achievements count.
Practice Calendar (a year, GitHub-style). Consistency: days practised,
sessions this week, sessions per week, sessions in 30 days, busiest day,
average focus, average gap between sessions, longest gap. Your practice
breakdown by category.

**Goals** — Weekly goal, with Edit. A goals list with + Add goal. That is all.

**Roadmap** — All 8 levels with their hour bands and grade equivalents
(First Steps 0–50h · before Grade 1 … Artist 3,500h+ · LRSM to FRSM), the
statues, the objectives inside each level, and the four area pins.

**History** — 77 sessions, itemised: every logged item with its duration,
including tool sessions and course lessons. A Report.

**Leaderboard** — Last month's champions, and an opt-in toggle.

**Achievements** — 104 / 181 unlocked. Closest to unlocking. Categories:
Your Journey 8/8, Practice 33/81, Courses 3/7, Piano Practice Daily 3/4,

**Pieces** — All Pieces / Books / My Collection, search, Currently Learning,

## 2 · The problem, stated plainly

**The dashboard restates eight other places.** Your Path and This level are
Roadmap. At a glance and All time are Stats. Weekly practice is Goals.
Leaderboard positions is Leaderboard. The activity feed is Achievements and
Community. Courses is Learn. Practice tools is Tools. From Matt and Go deeper
are Learn and Live.

That is why it is 5,858px long, and why it is the weakest page: it is not a
page in its own right, it is a summary of the whole product. A summary of
everything is a directory, and the nav is already the directory.

**Two tabs are underweight.** Goals holds one setting and a list. Leaderboard
holds one table and a toggle.

**One tab is doing the real work quietly.** Roadmap already holds the whole
level system, the hour bands, the grade equivalents, the objectives per level
and the four area levels. It is the most substantial page in the section and
the dashboard duplicates its headline while burying its detail.

## 3 · One question per tab

The test for whether something belongs somewhere: each tab answers exactly one
question a member actually asks.

| Tab | The question | Register |
|---|---|---|
| **Dashboard** | *What do I do now — and did what I did count?* | Now |
| **Pieces** | *What shall I play?* | Choosing |
| **Roadmap** | *Where am I going, and what am I aiming for?* | Direction |
| **Progress** | *What have I actually done?* | Record |
| **Goals** | *What have I set myself?* | Intention |

Nothing on a tab that does not answer its question. Anything answering another
tab's question is a link, not a section.

---

## 4 · The split

### Dashboard — *what do I do now*
- Where you stand: the distance to your next level, the statue, the ladder.
- The act: Log practice.
- What it added up to: streak, the week, the weekly goal.
- **Today's five**: piece, sight-reading, scales, theory, one tool.
- One line of direction: which area is behind, and what to aim for about it.
- The room: a few lines of what other members have just done.

Everything else becomes a link. No catalogues, no lists that continue
elsewhere, no restating another tab's headline.

### Pieces — *what shall I play*
Unchanged in scope: the library, books, your collection, currently learning.
It already knows what it is. It is the only tab that does.

### Roadmap — *where am I going*
Gains the weight the dashboard was borrowing:
- The eight levels, hour bands, grade equivalents, statues. **Already there.**
- **The four area levels, in full** — the long-term bars, the hours behind
  them, and what a weakness means. This is their proper home. They are
  cumulative and diagnostic; they belong with the level system that defines
  them, not on a screen you check for twenty seconds.
- **All 16 objectives for the current level**, grouped by area, with the
  completed ones. This is the direction page.

### Progress — *what have I done*
Absorbs Stats, History, Leaderboard and Achievements, which are four views of
one question:
- The practice calendar and the consistency figures.
- The breakdown by category.
- The session history.
- Achievements (104/181, closest to unlocking).
- The leaderboard, opt-in, as one view among several rather than a tab.

### Goals — *what have I set myself*
The open one. It currently holds a weekly target and a list. Either it grows
into something real — targets per area, a target date for the next level,
pieces you mean to finish — or it folds into the dashboard as the weekly line
it already is, and the tab goes. **I would fold it**, and make Practice four
tabs: Dashboard, Pieces, Roadmap, Progress. Four strong tabs beat five where
one is thin.

---

## 5 · What this decides about the dashboard

With that split, the dashboard carries **three things**: where you stand, the
day's five, and one line of direction. Plus the room.

That is sparse. Which settles the question that has been blocking everything:

> **Dark is affordable, because the page is short and its type is large.**

The legibility cost of a dark ground is small at large sizes and grows as type
shrinks. A three-part page can be dark without paying for it. A fourteen-part
page cannot, which is exactly why the live page could never have been dark.

And Learn and Tools keep what makes them feel like events — the full-bleed
artwork, the hue per item, the cinematic hero. The dashboard shares the ground
without borrowing the theatre.

---

## 6 · Still open

1. **Goals as a tab.** Fold it, or grow it. My recommendation is to fold it.
2. **Objectives on the dashboard.** They give direction, so they cannot be
   invisible — but sixteen is a catalogue, and there is no "next one" because
   members work on many at once. My recommendation: show the ones tagged to
   **the area that is behind**, which is usually two or three. That answers
   "which ones" with a reason rather than an arbitrary slice, and it ties the
   direction to the diagnosis instead of leaving them as two unrelated systems.
3. **The room.** Relatedness is a real driver, and this is the only place
   members see each other. Keep it small on the dashboard, or give it a proper
   home in Community and link.
