# First Steps: course content draft (Pieces 1 to 4)

Draft of the first four pieces for tone and difficulty review, before I write the remaining eight and before anything is engraved or recorded. Each piece has a plain-language spec (what to engrave and play) and its explainer (what the learner reads).

House rules followed: "stave" not "staff", "practice" with a c, no "met/meet", no em dashes, no emojis, reading in step with Theory Level 1.

A note on the notes: everything below stays in the middle C position (both thumbs able to reach middle C), uses only stepwise motion until Piece 5, and only quarter, half and whole notes until later. Fingering is written as finger numbers (1 = thumb).

---

## Piece 1 — Finding Your Way

**Skill:** getting comfortable. Posture, a relaxed hand and wrist, finger numbers, finding your way around the keys, and keeping a steady pulse. No notation yet.

**Spec (no stave):** a guided exploration rather than a written piece, so the page shows a keyboard diagram, not notation.
- Sit tall, arms loose, hands resting lightly curved as if holding a small ball.
- Finger numbers: thumbs are 1, then 2, 3, 4, 5 out to the little finger, the same on both hands.
- The black keys come in groups of two and groups of three. Play a two-black-key group with fingers 2 and 3, and a three-black-key group with fingers 2, 3 and 4. Do it in a few places up and down the piano.
- Find C: it is the white key just to the left of every two-black-key group. Play every C up the keyboard.
- Find middle C: the C nearest the middle of the piano. This is home base for everything that follows.
- Keep a pulse: play middle C slowly and evenly to a steady count of "1, 2, 3, 4", over and over, relaxed, letting the arm drop the note rather than pressing.

**Audio:** a short demonstration of the two- and three-black-key groups, then a steady, even middle C pulse at a calm tempo.

**Explainer:**

> **New:** before any notes, we get comfortable. How you sit and hold your hands now is what everything else is built on.
>
> **How:** sit tall with your arms loose, hands lightly curved as if each is holding a small ball. Your thumbs are finger 1, counting out to 5 at the little finger, the same on both hands.
>
> **Find your way:** the black keys sit in groups of two and three. That pattern is your map. The white key just to the left of any two-black-key group is a C. Play every C up the piano, then find the one nearest the middle. That is middle C, your home base from here on.
>
> **Keep the pulse:** play middle C slowly to a steady "1, 2, 3, 4". Do not press. Let the weight of your arm drop the note, and keep the count even. A steady pulse is worth more right now than any fast playing.
>
> **How to practice:** spend a minute just finding Cs, then a minute keeping that even pulse. If your hand or shoulder feels tight, stop and shake it loose. Comfort first.
>
> **Next:** we put the right-hand thumb on middle C and read our first notes.

## Piece 2 — Middle C

**Skill:** playing single notes with the right hand, and reading up by step from the middle C landmark.

**Spec:**
- Clef: treble. Position: right-hand fingers 1, 2, 3 on C (middle C), D, E.
- Time: 4/4. Note values: quarter and half only. Stepwise, no skips.
- Notes and fingering (RH):
  - Bar 1: C (1) quarter, D (2) quarter, E (3) half
  - Bar 2: E (3) quarter, D (2) quarter, C (1) half
  - Bar 3: C (1) quarter, D (2) quarter, E (3) quarter, D (2) quarter
  - Bar 4: C (1) half, C (1) half
- Ends on middle C.

**Audio:** right hand only, steady and singing, at a calm tempo.

**Explainer:**

> **New:** your first note to play, and your anchor. Middle C is the note you always find your bearings from, on the keyboard and on the page.
>
> **How:** right-hand thumb on middle C, hand lightly curved, wrist loose. Let the weight of the arm play the note rather than pressing with the finger.
>
> **Reading:** every note here is middle C or a step above it. Read by shape, one step up at a time, so your fingers climb with the notes: 1, 2, 3. If you cannot place a note yet, say its name out loud, using the landmarks and mnemonics from Theory Level 1.
>
> **Keep the pulse:** count out loud, a steady "1, 2, 3, 4", so the beat stays even and the half notes get their full two counts.
>
> **How to practice:** play it slowly, find the one spot your hand hesitates, and play just that spot a few times before you run the whole thing again. That is practice. Playing it top to bottom over and over is not. Struggling with the timing? Prioritise counting out loud. Struggling with the notes? Prioritise saying their names.
>
> **Next:** the left hand does the same thing, one octave lower.

## Piece 3 — Left Hand Joins

**Skill:** the left hand, reading down from middle C into the bass, and the whole note.

**Spec:**
- Clef: bass. Position: left-hand fingers 1, 2, 3 on C (middle C), B, A, going down.
- Time: 4/4. Note values: quarter, half, and the new whole note (hold four counts).
- Notes and fingering (LH):
  - Bar 1: C (1) quarter, B (2) quarter, A (3) half
  - Bar 2: A (3) quarter, B (2) quarter, C (1) half
  - Bar 3: C (1) quarter, B (2) quarter, A (3) quarter, B (2) quarter
  - Bar 4: C (1) whole
- Ends on middle C, held for four.

**Audio:** left hand only, calm and even, holding the final whole note its full length.

**Explainer:**

> **New:** the left hand, and a note you hold for a long time. The whole note lasts four full counts.
>
> **How:** left-hand thumb on middle C, then fingers 2 and 3 step down to B and A. Same relaxed, curved hand as the right, just moving the other way.
>
> **Reading:** this piece is written in the bass clef, the lower stave, and it steps down from middle C. Middle C sits just below the treble stave and just above the bass stave, so it is the note that joins the two, exactly as Theory Level 1 shows it. Read by shape, one step down at a time, or say each name out loud if you need to.
>
> **Keep the pulse:** count "1, 2, 3, 4" out loud. On the last bar, play middle C on count 1 and hold it, still counting all four, so the whole note gets its full length. Holding a note is as much a part of the beat as playing one.
>
> **How to practice:** if the whole note keeps getting cut short, count out loud and watch your hand stay down until you reach 4. Isolate that last bar and repeat just it.
>
> **Next:** both hands together for the first time.

## Piece 4 — Mirror Image

**Skill:** hands together for the first time, in the gentlest form. Both hands start on middle C and move outward as a mirror, using the same fingers.

**Spec:**
- Grand stave. Both thumbs (finger 1) on middle C. Right hand steps up C-D-E (fingers 1-2-3), left hand steps down C-B-A (fingers 1-2-3) at the same time, so the hands mirror each other with matching fingers.
- Time: 4/4. Note values: quarter and half. Both hands play the same rhythm throughout.
- Notes and fingering (RH / LH move together):
  - Bar 1: C+C (1/1) quarter, D+B (2/2) quarter, E+A (3/3) half
  - Bar 2: E+A (3/3) quarter, D+B (2/2) quarter, C+C (1/1) half
  - Bar 3: C+C (1/1) quarter, D+B (2/2) quarter, E+A (3/3) quarter, D+B (2/2) quarter
  - Bar 4: C+C (1/1) half, C+C (1/1) half
- Both hands begin and end on middle C.

**Audio:** both hands, slow and even, so the mirror is clearly heard.

**Explainer:**

> **New:** both hands at once. This is the gentlest possible version, because your hands are mirror images: they use the same fingers and move in opposite directions from the same starting note.
>
> **How:** put both thumbs on middle C. When the right hand steps up to D, the left hand steps down to B, with the same finger in each hand. Your hands open outward and close back in together, like a butterfly.
>
> **Reading:** the right-hand notes are on the top stave, the left-hand notes on the bottom stave, joined at middle C in the middle. You already read both of these lines in the last two pieces, so nothing here is new to read. The only new thing is doing them together.
>
> **Keep the pulse:** count "1, 2, 3, 4" out loud and let it hold both hands together. If one hand rushes ahead, the count is what pulls it back.
>
> **How to practice:** hands separately first, right hand until it is easy, then left hand until it is easy. Only then put them together, and go slower than feels necessary. If they tangle, your hands are ahead of you: halve the speed and count out loud. Speed comes later and on its own.
>
> **Next:** we add our first skip, stepping past a note instead of to the note beside it.
