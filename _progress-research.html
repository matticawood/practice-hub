What our data really is

Four facts about the practice log that change every figure computed from it. All four were missed in the first draft of this document because it read the first thousand rows of tables that run to thousands.

Only 47% of "sessions" are practice

practice_sessions holds 3,647 rows, but the source column splits them: manual 1,726, app-lesson 1,056, app-game 710, app-article 110, quick 45. The majority are the app logging a lesson, a game or an article, not somebody sitting down at the piano.

By minutes it inverts. Manual practice is 85,327 of 102,543 minutes, 83% of all the time. So anything counted by row — sessions per week, average session, the session-length distribution, the busiest day — is badly distorted, and anything summed by minute is sound.

One lesson writes items into four areas

An app-lesson session writes several practice_items, and they land in different areas: theory, piece, technique and sight-reading from the same lesson. So the four-area split at the top of the page is partly an artefact of lesson auto-logging rather than a record of what anybody chose to work on. This needs settling before that chapter can be trusted.

The log is four months old

Every row falls between 20 April and 28 August 2026. The first draft reported "median three months of history, maximum five" as a fact about members. It is a fact about the table. Nobody can have twelve months of history because the log has not existed for twelve months, so every twelve-month axis on the page is empty by construction rather than by behaviour.

A session's minutes are the sum of its items

True in 3,642 of 3,644 cases. Minutes can be summed across sessions and items without double counting, which is the one thing that makes the time figures trustworthy.

The difficulties members name

symptom_vocab holds 26 difficulties in 10 families, written in the player's own voice — "my hands won't work together", "I can play it slowly but it collapses when I speed up", "everything comes out the same volume" — and each one carries a split_cat mapping it to one of the four areas: technique 8, musicianship 6, repertoire 5, sight-reading 4.

The row counts flatter it badly and the honest position is narrower. 41 members have named a real difficulty at least once, 19 have named three or more. The most common are I struggle to sight read (26), I don't understand how chords work (19), I don't know what to do with the pedal (17) and my hands won't work together (17). Signals arrive from session notes, comments, chat, posts, bios and sheet notes — eighteen sources in all.

And one thing nobody is shown

user_collections.status includes aspire — 292 rows across 78 members. Three quarters of the membership have told us what they want to play one day, and no page reflects it back. It is the single largest piece of stated intent in the database.

The right comparables

Not practice loggers. Products where several capacities must be developed together over years, which is what this app is for. A first draft of this study leant on Modacity, Pract.is and paper practice journals, and concluded the page should organise around the piece. That was wrong: those products serve players who arrive with a piece and a teacher, and their organising principle does not transfer to an app whose job is the whole of playing.

ProductThe problem it solvesWhat it does that we could
Garmin
Training Load Focus		 Three training capacities that must stay in balance. A single status word — Balanced, X focus, X shortage — instead of four numbers. Lichess & Chess.com

Keybr Motor skill with per-element weakness. The analysis feeds straight back into what you are given next. Weak keys appear more often. Analytics as an input, not a report.
Khan Academy
mastery levels		 Hundreds of skills, partial knowledge. Four named states — Not started, Familiar, Proficient, Mastered — and a course percentage that counts only Proficient and above. Mastery as a grid of darkening cells.
Anki
FSRS, true retention		 Knowledge that decays. Separates desired from true retention, and reports young and mature material separately because they behave differently. Its own documentation warns that a single day is never a good indicator and to read monthly.
Duolingo Thousands of items, all decaying. Per-item strength with a decay rate set by how well you actually know it. New material fades in days, well-known material in months.

ABRSM The syllabus our roadmap mirrors. Every grade assesses four strands together — pieces, scales and arpeggios, sight-reading, aural — out of 150 points, from the earliest grade. Holistic by construction, and the reason our four areas exist.
Spotify Wrapped
and Strava Year in Sport		 Data nobody would otherwise look at. A periodic narrative rather than a permanent dashboard. The lesson is that some analysis belongs in a moment, not on a page.

What they agree on

Every one of them names a state rather than only reporting a number. Balanced or short. Familiar or proficient. Young or mature. The number is there, but the verdict is what carries.

And every one of them refuses to speak when it has too little data. Garmin will not give a verdict under seven days. Anki tells you to read monthly. That is a discipline our page does not currently have.

What the evidence says to measure

This decides which of our numbers deserve the top of the page. Hours is not the winner.

  • Deliberate practice accounts for roughly a quarter of the skill difference between musicians.Macnamara & Hambrick
  • Practice has to be deliberate, not merely accumulated.Ericsson
  • Focus degrades after 15–25 minutes; distributed beats massed.practice science
  • Specific goals beat vague ones.goal-setting research
  • Competence needs clear progression and immediate corrective feedback.self-determination theory
  • Points and badges used as controls undermine intrinsic interest; the same mechanics used as competence feedback sustain it.SDT in app design
  • Comparison against a target slightly above you inspires. Against an unreachable one it produces envy and demotivation.social comparison research
  • A metric that changes no decision is vanity. Once it becomes the target it stops measuring anything.Goodhart

Read against our page

The literature names points, badges, login streaks and totals as the classic vanity set that rarely correlates with mastery. Our page leads with total hours and carries streaks and achievements. The measures the evidence actually favours — session length against the 15–25 minute window, the spacing between sessions, the focus rating, the named difficulty, progress on a specific piece — are all present in the database and all currently shown as a single tile, a hidden panel, or not at all.

On comparison, our leaderboards show full ranking. The research is specific that a target slightly above the individual outperforms full distribution visibility, and that downward movement should either reset quickly, stay private, or come with a visible way back.

How each should be drawn

Chart choice is not taste. Cleveland and McGill measured how accurately people read each visual channel, and the ranking decides which of our charts work.

For reading quantities: position on a common scale first, then length, then angle, then area, with colour intensity near the bottom. Stephen Few adds that sparklines and bullet graphs replace gauges, and that 3D slows comprehension by up to 30%.

The four areas as a shareFour full-width bars, each on its own scaleWrongA part-to-whole where nothing shares a scale, so the bars can be compared neither with each other nor with the total. One stacked bar, or a dot plot on a shared axis.

The four areas over timeFilled total with four lines, and a stacked variantBy widthStacked areas have a moving baseline so middle bands cannot be read; lines compare better. Four series is above the two or three a phone can carry, so small multiples there.

When you practise24 hourly barsBin on mobileFive to seven bars is the readable limit on a phone. 24 is texture.
Hours by weekday7 bars, each stacked four waysDrop the stackSeven bars is right; the four-way split leaves roughly one observation per cell at our volumes.
Session lengthsFive bars with a benchmarkBest on the pageA distribution on a common scale with a reference line. Should be manual rows only.
Tool scoresFigure plus sparklineRightExactly Few's recommendation.
Consistency figuresSix numbers, no chartRightA glance value wants a number. This is what a first tier is.
The four areas as a shape—NeverThe obvious temptation is a radar chart. Its area grows as the square of the value so small differences look large, the axis order is arbitrary and changes the shape, and comparing non-adjacent axes needs a mental rotation people are bad at.

Colour

Our four area colours are gold, indigo, green and purple. Green against gold and indigo against purple are the two pairs most at risk under deuteranopia and protanopia, and every chart on the page currently distinguishes the four areas by colour alone. The rule is that colour must never be the only channel: pair it with a label, a position or a pattern. Any palette should be checked against protanopia, deuteranopia and tritanopia simulations, and the Okabe–Ito and IBM palettes are the accepted safe sets.

On a phone

Five to seven bars, two to three series, larger type, thicker strokes. A tap opens a tooltip and a tap elsewhere closes it, and the tooltip must be readable without zooming. Range selectors and tab strips belong low on the screen where a thumb reaches. Our charts are currently the desktop drawing scaled down, which the guidance names explicitly as the thing not to do.

When there is nothing to show

More than half the membership has never logged practice by hand, and this is the state most of them will meet.

The first-run empty state decides whether somebody comes back, and the guidance is consistent: never show a blank chart. Show what the populated view will look like, give one obvious next action, and reveal complexity only as it is earned. Carbon and Nielsen Norman both treat the empty state as an onboarding moment rather than an error.

Set against that, our position is worse than it looks. Chapters currently hide themselves when empty, so a member with three sessions sees a page that is mostly gaps with no explanation of what would fill them. And with a median of 47 practice days across 3 months for even our most active members, several of our breakdowns sit under the threshold where a trend means anything: below about 30 observations the usual approximations stop holding and any apparent pattern is mostly noise.

The discipline to borrow

Garmin will not give a verdict under 7 days of data and withholds detailed targets under 4 weeks. Anki's own documentation says a single day is never a good indicator and to read monthly. Both would rather say "not yet" than say something unreliable. Every chapter on our page should know its own minimum and say so plainly when it is not met.

The constraints, in order

What any design has to satisfy, before taste enters.

One

Separate chosen practice from auto-logged app activity, or fix the definition. Half the rows and a fifth of the minutes are not somebody sitting down to play, and one lesson currently writes into four areas at once.

Two

Fit every window to four months of history, and revisit when there is more. Nothing on this page can honestly carry a twelve-month axis yet.

Three

Name a state, do not only report a number. Balanced, short, familiar, proficient, stale. The comparables all do this and it is the difference between a record and a reading.

Four

Give every chapter a minimum and let it say "not yet" instead of drawing something unreliable.

Five

Lead with what the evidence says predicts improvement — length, spacing, focus, named difficulty, specific goals — rather than with the totals the literature calls vanity.

Six

Three tiers on separate surfaces, not thirteen chapters in one scroll. The median visit is 56 seconds.

Seven

Use the layer that already exists. Tier and its reason, pieces finished per tier, aspiration, symptom weighting and theory level are computed and unused.
