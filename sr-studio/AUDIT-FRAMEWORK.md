# The Audit Framework — every facet a piece can be right or wrong on

The complete attack surface for a two-hand tonal keyboard piece (ABRSM 2-4). Auditing means
**looking deeply and weighing each facet in context** — what is right in one situation is wrong in
another (a soft high ending is morendo in an Adagio, unfinished in a march). Never a glance, never a
checkbox. Each item below is a thing to actually evaluate, with its failure mode. Governed by the three
criteria: composer-thinking, preference-based, fully reasoned.

## I. Melody (the horizontal line)
- Contour: one coherent shaped gesture; a single clear high point (unless the character wants otherwise); peak approached and quit properly.
- Range: readable/singable register; grade-appropriate width; no ledger extremes.
- Interval content: step/leap balance for the character; no un-idiomatic leaps (aug 2nd/4th, 7ths); leaps recovered; no leap into a dissonance.
- Tendency-tone resolution in the line: leading tone rises to ^1, ^4->^3, ^6->^5, chromatics resolve.
- Non-harmonic tones: every weak note a real role (passing/neighbour/suspension/appoggiatura/escape/anticipation), prepared AND resolved as that role requires (a passing tone LEFT by step, not a leap).
- Repeated notes / stasis: purposeful, not droning, not circling, not a repeated tendency-tone.
- Direction: not jagged, not monotone; turns lead somewhere.
- Opening & closing gestures: which degree, anacrusis vs downbeat; resolves to ^1 by step/small leap.
- Freshness: composed, not a note-spinner or cliche.

## II. Harmony (vertical + progression)
- Functional syntax: T-PD-D-T; dominant resolves; no aimless retrogression (V->IV); no I-V seesaw.
- Vocabulary vs grade: diatonic 2-3; secondary dominants only at 4.
- Harmonic rhythm: appropriate rate, varied, independent of melodic density.
- Cadences: right type at each phrase end, PAC final; approached correctly; not hollow.
- Key establishment: tonic grounded before colour; minor/relative-major ambiguity resolved.
- Chord legibility: root present/implied; not a rootless voicing.
- Inversions: bass chosen for the line; no unprepared/unresolved 6/4; dim in first inversion.
- Doubling: never the leading tone, never a dissonance.
- Minor ^6/^7: by purpose (harmonic when chordal, melodic when a line); no cross-relation between hands.
- The melody over a tonicisation: reflects the chromatic (a V/V wants C#, not a diatonic C nat).
- Colour/interest: enough distinct chords; the departure has real interest.

## III. Counterpoint (melody vs bass = the outer frame)
- Independence: contrary/oblique preferred; two lines, not one doubled.
- Parallel perfects: none on ANY beat (onset AND offbeat); no hidden/direct 5ths/8ves.
- Spacing: outer voices not colliding, no unmanaged gap.
- Simultaneities: on-beat consonance unless a prepared/resolved dissonance; an exposed outer tritone (dim chord) must resolve.
- The bass as a line: its own contour, not a row of roots.

## IV. Rhythm & metre
- Metre fits character; tempo mark matches.
- Rhythmic motif: a germ, developed, not re-stamped nor all-different.
- Variety vs monotony; character of the rhythm carries the feel.
- Syncopation purposeful and resolved.
- Rests: real function, correctly placed and notated.
- Notatability: legal values, nothing crosses a beat un-tied, ties/dots/beaming show the beat.
- Anacrusis arithmetic balances the final bar.
- Two-hand rhythmic relationship: some independence, coordinated.

## V. Texture & voicing
- Texture fits character; consistent, not randomly switching (a lone single note amid two-voice chords is a hole).
- Figuration idiom correctness (alberti/oom-pah/walking/broken/sustained).
- LH chord spacing: close and idiomatic; no awkward wide leaps (third->sixth tread); no muddy low thirds.
- Voice count vs grade cap.
- Register separation; bass not muddy-low.
- Accompaniment supports not masks; inner voice has life where held.
- Breathing: responds to the melody's phrase ends.

## VI. Form & structure
- Phrase structure (period/sentence/binary) coherent; lengths regular or purposefully asymmetric.
- Balance & proportion.
- Repetition vs variation balance.
- Climax placed in the form (2nd half, once, before the final cadence) — unless the character sets it elsewhere.
- A real beginning/middle/end trajectory.

## VII. Motif & development
- A germ exists with profile (not a drone/flat run).
- It recurs (unity).
- It develops (sequence/inversion/fragmentation/aug-dim); consequent contrasts antecedent.
- Development, not literal copy-paste; identity not lost.

## VIII. Fingering & pianism
- Reachability: every finger plays its note from the prior one.
- Position logic: sensible positions; real, minimal, logical shifts; reserve fingers for coming extremes.
- No jams/slides: no same-finger legato slide, no weak-finger jam on a skip, no thumb-on-black in a scale.
- Consistency: a recurring pitch/shape keeps its finger (NOT G4=1 then G4=2).
- Opening finger: a sensible anchor for the position.
- Marking: ABRSM-sparse but complete; a mark at every genuine position change/crossing/shape change; none on inferable notes; every printed number playable and not contradicted by the next.
- At tempo; grade-appropriate demands.

## IX. Dynamics
- Level fits character & grade band.
- Coherent arc; one climax; soft ends.
- Hairpin vs terraced matches character; direction always correct.
- Placement at phrase starts / the climax; a mark only where the level changes.
- Present (grade requires); not over/under-marked; never drawn through a both-hands rest.
- Cross-check against the melodic/harmonic climax — divergence can be a deliberate morendo OR a mistake; weigh it.

## X. Articulation & phrasing
- Slurs = real phrases, balanced with the melodic phrase lengths.
- Staccato only on short, detached-character notes; playable at tempo; not on dotted/lilt or long values.
- Character match; both hands agree where sharing.
- Grade features (tenuto/accent/fermata at g4) with a cause.
- Nothing contradictory.

## XI. Notation & engraving
- Spelling: leading tone sharp not flat; chromatics spelled by resolution AND by the local chord (a secondary dominant's own accidentals).
- Accidentals correct, not missing/redundant.
- Ties/rests/beaming metre-correct; beat visible.
- Key/time/clef correct; fingering placement correct.
- Legible at sight.

## XII. Grade & pedagogy
- Canvas limits (range, note-floor, keys, metres, chord size) within grade.
- Uniform difficulty; no freak-hard bar; sight-readable throughout.
- Introduces grade features appropriately.

## XIII. Character & aesthetic coherence (the whole)
- One coherent character; every layer serves it; tempo/title match the notes.
- Cross-domain agreement: dynamic/melodic/harmonic/textural peaks are (or deliberately are not) the same moment.
- Naturalness: nothing mechanical.
- Distinctness within the set.
- Musicality: would a musician be glad to play and hear it.
